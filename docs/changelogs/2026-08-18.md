### Check 1 — Fabrication

**Variant A: one clear, severe instance, structural in origin.**
Leona's Variant A `reframe_teaser.why_bullets`:

> "You redesigned the receiving workflow in six weeks, clearing a backlog
> that had stalled the entire site's intake for months."

Leona's real `discovery_answers`/evidence_units contain nothing about a
"receiving workflow," a "backlog," or a "site's intake" — nothing
resembling this at all. This sentence is not a paraphrase of anything in
her evidence. It is a **near-verbatim copy of `LAYER_2_PROMPT`'s own
worked example** for the why_bullets word-count rule (`lib/prompts/identity-report.ts`
line ~571: *"Extended (20 words, same fact plus its stakes/consequence):
'You redesigned the receiving workflow in six weeks, clearing a backlog
that had stalled the entire site's intake for months.'"*). The model
pulled the prompt's own illustrative example into a real user's report as
if it were her material. This is a previously undocumented, confirmed
fabrication vector specific to *having* a worked example in the prompt —
not something Variant B can reproduce, because Variant B has no worked
example to copy from.

**Variant B: fabrication of a different kind — implying evidence that
doesn't exist rather than inventing evidence content.** Katalin (0
secondary signatures given) — Variant B `secondary_signature_summary`:

> "While your primary signatures define your core identity, your
> secondary signatures provide additional insights into your preferences
> and tendencies."

Nadia (also 0 secondary signatures given) — Variant B:

> "While your primary signatures are dominant, your secondary signatures
> provide additional layers to your identity, enhancing your ability to
> adapt and thrive in various environments."

Both assert that secondary signatures exist and add value, when the
`secondary_signature_analysis` array fed in was empty for both. This is
2/4 of this run's zero-secondary personas (Leona and Ingrid's Variant B
correctly stated "no secondary signatures were identified" — see Check 3).
Variant A got the zero-case right 4/4 (Katalin, Nadia, Leona, Ingrid all
correctly state no additional pattern surfaced with sufficient evidence).

### Check 2 — Ungrounded confidence

Both variants assert primary/secondary signature confidence levels and
core statements with equal declarative force regardless of underlying
evidence thinness — this is inherent to the schema (a fixed `confidence`
field) rather than a prompt-wording difference, so no variant-level
distinction was observed here beyond what Check 3 already captures more
concretely. Not scored as a separate finding.

### Check 3 — Thin-evidence overconfidence

**Variant A fabricates padding for thin-evidence *primary* signatures —
a failure mode with zero code backstop**, unlike secondaries
(`enforceSecondaryEvidenceFloor` only guards secondaries). Katalin's
Pioneer signature has exactly **one** evidence_unit: *"I find repetition
much harder than complexity."* Variant A's `evidence_analysis`:

> "Your pioneering spirit is evident in your willingness to explore new
> paths, even when they are complex and uncertain. You find repetition
> much harder than complexity, indicating a preference for innovation
> over routine. **This was demonstrated in your approach to tackling new
> challenges, where you sought out opportunities to innovate and
> improve.** This matters because it allows you to push boundaries..."

The bolded sentence has no source — nothing in Katalin's evidence_units
describes her "tackling new challenges" or "seeking out opportunities to
innovate." It's invented to satisfy the "minimum 2 story anchors" /
Pattern→Evidence→Meaning structure on a signature that only had one real
anchor. Variant B's version of the same signature stayed closer to the
literal evidence, with no invented elaboration:

> "You find repetition challenging and are drawn to exploring new paths
> and ideas."

Terser, but not fabricated — an incidental side effect of B having no
"minimum 2 anchors" instruction pressuring it to pad. This is not a
universal pattern (checked Tomasz's 1-evidence Meaning Maker primary in
both variants; both stayed within defensible paraphrase of the single
real quote), but Katalin's case is a clean, real instance and flags a gap
in the live pipeline's defenses — the floor/anchor rule that exists to
produce depth has no corresponding code check the way secondaries do.

**Zero-secondary-signature handling** (4 of 6 personas had none):
Variant A: 4/4 correct, confident "no additional pattern surfaced"
language, matching the prompt's explicit zero-case instruction. Variant
B: 2/4 correct (Leona: *"No secondary signatures were identified in this
analysis."* — Ingrid: *"...there are no secondary signatures identified
in this analysis."*), 2/4 fabricated existence (Katalin, Nadia — see
Check 1). Without the explicit zero-case instruction, Variant B is
roughly a coin flip on this specific defect.

### Check 4 — Toothless teaser

**The single cleanest, most deterministic finding in this whole test.**
Variant B's `reframe_teaser.reframe` field opens with "Consider how..."
in **6 of 6 personas**, every one resolving into directional advice
rather than an open tension — exactly the failure mode the live prompt's
No Coaching Rule and reframe-specific "must end on a live, open tension,
not a resolved or reassuring statement" instruction exist to prevent:

- Katalin: "Consider how your unique blend of visioning and driving can
  be leveraged to lead transformative projects and initiatives."
- Matteo: "Consider how you can leverage your strengths in new contexts
  or industries that value innovation and transformation."
- Nadia: "Consider how you can leverage your strengths in building and
  activating to create environments that not only function well but also
  fulfill your need for purpose."
- Tomasz: "Consider how you can balance your drive for action with the
  need for reflection and strategic planning."
- Leona: "Consider how you can leverage your pioneering spirit and
  contextual awareness to drive innovation and meaningful change."
- Ingrid: "Consider how you can leverage your deep expertise and
  craftsmanship to influence and mentor others, expanding your impact
  beyond individual projects."

Variant A's reframe field never does this — all 6 stay in the
descriptive/tension register the prompt asks for (e.g. Katalin: *"Yet,
the systems you've built no longer need active tending, leaving a
question of what's next."*). Variant A is not clean, however: **2 of 6
directly violate the live prompt's own explicit hard rules** — Nadia's
reframe contains the explicitly banned word "gap" (*"...leaving a gap in
your drive for purpose."*), and Ingrid's reframe ends in a question mark
(*"Yet, the question remains: what new challenges will reignite your
passion and expertise?"*), which the prompt explicitly forbids ("never a
question, never ending in a question mark"). So Variant A's rule
compliance here is real but imperfect (~67%), while Variant B's failure
on the same dimension is total and uniform (0/6) — a difference in kind,
not just degree.

**why_bullets evidence-grounding** shows the same split. Variant A's
bullets, even when short, stay anchored to real specifics (Tomasz: *"You
led a temporary crisis unit, rerouting cargo across several countries
under pressure."*). Variant B's bullets frequently become generic,
ungrounded platitudes with no citation to any real fact — Tomasz's
Variant B why_bullets:

> "Balancing action with reflection can enhance decision-making."
> "Strategic planning can lead to more sustainable outcomes."
> "Reflection can provide deeper insights and innovation."

None of these name a specific fact, moment, or quote from Tomasz's actual
answers — this is the exact failure the prompt's "if a bullet could be
true of this identity in the abstract without citing anything the user
actually said, rewrite it" instruction exists to prevent. Same pattern in
Leona's Variant B (*"Your ability to adapt and explore new paths is a key
strength."*) and Ingrid's Variant B (*"Mentoring can provide a sense of
purpose and legacy."*).

### Check 5 — Cross-section repetition

**Variant A is not clean here either — 3 of 6 personas show severe,
near-verbatim overlap between `cover.identity_thesis` and
`constellation_synthesis`**, the exact failure `enforceConstellationSynthesisNonOverlap`
exists to catch in the live pipeline (not run in this standalone test, so
this is raw, pre-backstop model output — see Methodology note below).

- Katalin — thesis: *"You transform neglected systems into valuable,
  lasting structures through vision and action."* Synthesis opens:
  *"You consistently transform neglected systems into valuable, lasting
  structures, driven by a vision for what could be."* — an 8+ word
  run repeated almost verbatim.
- Matteo — thesis: *"You transform neglected structures into valuable
  assets, driven by a need for tangible impact."* Synthesis opens: *"You
  thrive in environments where you can transform neglected spaces into
  valuable assets, driven by a need for tangible impact."* — identical
  but for "structures"→"spaces."
- Nadia — thesis: *"You transform chaotic environments into functional
  systems, driven by tangible results."* Synthesis opens: *"You
  consistently transform chaotic environments into functional systems,
  driven by a need for tangible results."* — near-identical.

Tomasz, Leona, and Ingrid's Variant A overlap was present but lighter
(shared words/theme, not full clause repetition). This corroborates the
existing doc comment on `enforceConstellationSynthesisNonOverlap` almost
exactly ("across 4 distinct prompt-wording attempts... at least one
sentence per run still lifted a 5+ word run verbatim") — the rule is
elaborately specified (an approved example, 3 named disqualified
patterns, an explicit "identify the thesis's verb and object" procedure)
and still fails on half of a fresh 6-persona sample.

**Variant B fails the same check in all 6 personas, and more severely —
including reproducing the prompt's own named "disqualified pattern"
verbatim, despite Variant B never having been told about it.** The live
prompt explicitly forbids opening constellation_synthesis with
"[possessive] identity as [Named Identity] is characterized by..." as a
named disqualified pattern. Variant B does exactly this in 5 of 6
personas:

- Katalin: *"Your identity as a 'Restorative Innovator' is characterized
  by a unique blend of visioning and driving..."*
- Nadia: *"Your identity is characterized by a strong drive to create and
  improve systems..."*
- Tomasz: *"Your identity as a Dynamic Problem Solver is characterized by
  your ability to quickly activate..."*
- Leona: *"As an Adaptive Explorer, you thrive in environments that allow
  you to explore new paths..."*
- Ingrid: *"As a Master Craftsman, you are driven by a desire to create
  enduring work..."*

And the underlying phrase-level overlap with identity_thesis is severe in
all 6, e.g. Ingrid — thesis: *"Your identity as a Master Craftsman is
defined by your dedication to precision and creating enduring work. You
excel in building lasting systems and diving deep into complex
problems..."* synthesis: *"As a Master Craftsman, you are driven by a
desire to create enduring work through precision and deep expertise.
Your ability to build lasting systems and solve complex problems..."* —
effectively a paraphrase-level duplicate of the same two sentences.
Variant A: 3/6 severe. Variant B: 6/6 severe, and reproducing the
prompt's own explicitly-named failure pattern with no prompting to do so.

*Methodology note:* neither variant had `enforceConstellationSynthesisNonOverlap`
or any other post-processing backstop applied in this test (both were
raw single-call outputs, matching the brief's "isolate the prompt
variable" design) — in the live pipeline, Variant A's 3 severe cases
above would have their offending sentence silently stripped before the
user sees it. This comparison is fair as a same-conditions test of
*prompt* behavior, but the live-pipeline delta for Variant A specifically
is smaller than the raw numbers here suggest, since code already
backstops this one rule for constellation_synthesis (though not for
`cover.identity_thesis` vs. anything else, and not for Variant B at all
since Variant B doesn't exist as a live path).

### Check 6 — Word-count/completeness

Covered quantitatively in the stats table above. Summary: the *numeric
floors* are already unenforced dead weight in Variant A (0/30
evidence_analysis fields clear 140 words; how_you_operate and
domain_profile_summary also frequently miss their floors) — stating a
number does not make the model hit that number. But the *qualitative*
instructions around those floors (Pattern→Evidence→Meaning structure,
"minimum 2 story anchors," "6–10" for energisers/friction_points, recap's
"~50-60 words, never fewer than 45" framing) are load-bearing for
*magnitude*: every field category collapses 3-6x under Variant B
regardless of specific floor compliance, and `energisers`/`friction_points`
in particular goes from a 7-10 range (roughly honoring the stated 6-10
spec) to a flat, deterministic 3/3 with no count instruction at all.

### Check 7 — General recognition quality

Qualitative read across all 12: Variant A consistently reads as
specific to the person — evidence_analysis paragraphs name concrete
circumstances (Ingrid's watchmaking/chronometer restoration; Tomasz's
crisis-unit cargo rerouting) even where the "matters because" clause
drifts toward generic interpretation. Variant B's primary-signature
write-ups are accurate paraphrases of the given evidence but read as
compressed labels more than lived narrative — e.g. Ingrid's Variant B
Builder entry ("Your experiences in watchmaking and restoration highlight
your focus on creating enduring work.") states the same fact Variant A's
version tells a story about, without any of the "identity must feel
lived, not labelled" quality the prompt's Writing Principle asks for.
Every Variant B `how_you_operate` field independently is 15-30 words —
functionally a one-line summary rather than a section a reader would
experience as reflective. Recognition quality favors Variant A across all
6 personas on this read, though not by as wide a margin as the raw word
counts suggest, since Variant B's shorter claims are rarely *wrong*, just
thin.

### Recommendation

**Not a clean win for wholesale prompt stripping.** The data supports a
specific, narrower conclusion:

- **Load-bearing, keep:** the per-field structural specs (`cover.identity_context`
  / `report_metadata` exact formats — Variant B leaked raw, third-person
  `identity_rationale` text into `cover.identity_context` in **6/6**
  personas, e.g. Tomasz's Variant B: *"The narrative consistently shows a
  pattern of engaging with complex, high-stakes situations..."* — a
  second-person-voice violation on a fixed product requirement, not a
  style choice); the zero-secondary-signature handling instruction (100%
  vs. 50% compliance); the "6-10" energisers/friction_points count; the
  evidence-grounding requirement on why_bullets/reframe ("must name a
  specific fact... not a restated trait"); the No Coaching framing for
  reframe (0/6 vs 6/6 "Consider how..." failures is the starkest single
  result in this test).
- **Dead weight or actively counterproductive, revise:** the exact
  numeric word floors (140/120/90/45/15) — never met by Variant A anyway,
  so restating them buys nothing; the why_bullets worked example
  specifically (produced a real fabrication in Leona's report by being
  copied instead of followed); the constellation_synthesis anti-overlap
  apparatus is the most interesting case — heavily specified (disqualified
  patterns, approved example, verb/object procedure) yet still fails on
  half of fresh real generations, meaning the current wording is not
  sufficient even though the underlying goal is clearly worth keeping
  (Variant B's 6/6 failure rate on the same axis show removing it
  entirely is worse, not better).
- **Not yet distinguishable from this data:** the Translation Formula,
  Specificity Rule, Repetition Rule, Frustration Inversion Rule, and
  Recognition Test didn't produce clean opposite-outcome evidence the way
  the checks above did — Variant A's extra length makes it *read* more
  specific, but that could be a length effect rather than these
  particular rules doing identifiable work.

**Next step:** a partially-minimal Variant C is warranted before any live
change — keep the per-field format specs, zero-case handling, array-count
specs, evidence-grounding/no-coaching framing for reframe, and *some*
form of the anti-overlap goal (worded differently, since the current
wording under-performs even when present); drop the exact numeric floors
and the why_bullets worked example. This is a prompt-design task, not
another blind test — Stage 0's job (find out whether the rule stack is
the problem) is answered: partially yes (some rules are genuinely inert
or harmful), partially no (several rules that look like boilerplate are
doing real, measurable work and their absence produces the single
cleanest failures in this entire test).

### Method actually run

**No — in every persona checked, the model either collapsed the three

  ensuring that your decisions are well-rounded and thoughtful. This

  ambiguous."* — three sentences, but the "situation" sentence ("careful

### Check 3 — Regression check

**Every previously-verified load-bearing behavior held — the regression
is confined to length, but it's broader than the one field this stage
was scoped to touch:**

| Behavior | Variant A | Variant C | Variant D |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reframe` avoids "Consider how..." | 0/6 | 0/6 | 0/6 |
| Zero-secondary handling correct | 2/2 | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| `why_bullets` evidence-grounded | 6/6 | 6/6 | 6/6 |
| `cover.identity_context` format correct | 6/6 | 6/6 | 6/6 |
| `evidence_analysis` avg words | 89.2 | 82.2 | **75.8 (new low)** |
| `how_you_operate` avg words | 81.2 | 52.2 | **45.1 (new low — the one field this stage targeted for improvement)** |

**New, unscoped finding — not something this stage was meant to fix, but
observed and logged per the stop condition ("log anything else as a
finding for a future stage"):** `energisers`/`friction_points` landed at
**exactly 6/6 in all 6 personas under Variant D** — the literal floor of
the spec's 6-10 range, with zero variation. Variant A ranged 6-9 across
personas; Variant C ranged 6-10. Both fields being pinned to the exact
same value, in every single persona, with no natural variation, is a
different failure signature than "sometimes hits the floor" — it reads
as the model applying a uniform compression to everything generated
after `how_you_operate`, not making 12 independent count judgments (2
fields × 6 personas) that happen to agree. Still technically spec-compliant
(6 is a valid value in 6-10), so not a defect on its own, but combined
with the `evidence_analysis` contraction above, the pattern suggests
adding substantial instructional text to `how_you_operate` — regardless
of what that text says — measurably compressed generation *elsewhere* in
the same response. This is a new hypothesis worth a dedicated future
check (e.g. does total system-prompt length correlate with completion
length independent of content), not something to chase down by rewording
Variant D further mid-test, per this stage's own stop condition.

### Recommendation

**Variant D is not ready and should not be proposed as the live change.**
It fails on its own stated goal — `how_you_operate` got shorter under D
than under C in 5 of 6 personas, moving further from Variant A's
~70-100 word/field range rather than closer to it — and the qualitative
read shows the "specific real situation" grounding the three-move
structure was designed to add didn't reliably appear either. On top of
that, it introduced a broader, unscoped contraction in `evidence_analysis`
and a suspiciously uniform `energisers`/`friction_points` count across
all 6 personas, neither of which this stage's change should have touched
at all.

Per this stage's own stop condition, the wording is not being iterated
further mid-test. **Next step is a new stage, not a patch to Variant D**:
before trying another wording change, investigate whether prompt length/
instructional density itself (independent of what the added text says)
is suppressing completion length elsewhere in the response — if that's
the mechanism, no amount of rewording `how_you_operate`'s spec will fix
it, and the fix belongs somewhere else entirely (e.g. reconsidering
`max_tokens`, generation order, or whether qualitative-scaffold framing
generalizes to `how_you_operate` at all vs. being specific to
`evidence_analysis`'s Pattern→Evidence→Meaning shape). Variant C — not
Variant D — remains the best-tested candidate from this whole #119
lineage so far, with its one known, now better-understood limitation
(the `how_you_operate` contraction from Stage 1) still unresolved.
